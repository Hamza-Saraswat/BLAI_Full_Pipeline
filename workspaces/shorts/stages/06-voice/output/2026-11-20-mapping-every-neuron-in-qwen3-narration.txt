Your chwen isn't a mystery anymore. Every neuron inside it got mapped. You run it in oh-lah-mah or L M studio, and when it does something weird, you shrug and re-prompt. Inside, your chwen is a stack of identical layers. Every word it writes climbs through every layer before the next word appears. Each layer is packed with neurons, the small calculators inside it. Until now, that whole climb was invisible to you. That map is built on one rude little move. A push means adding a fixed amount to one neuron's number at every word. No prompt. No fine-tune. One number, changed. Isn't poking a neuron just brain damage? Often, yes. A verified switch is a push that changes exactly one behaviour, like which language your copy answers in. It must beat a panel of matched random neurons and keep working on questions it has never seen. Fermion Research ran that test on every neuron. Picture a mixing desk: four hundred forty-two thousand faders, one per neuron. About one fader in a hundred does one clear job. The rest do nothing you can name. One hundred thirty-two faders are verified switches. One fader is labelled German. Unpushed, it writes German seven percent of the time. Pushed a single notch, it answers in German a hundred percent of the time. A couple of notches later, it babbles. That's the limit: faders are independent and linear, neurons interact. Push past the sweet spot and the whole desk squeals. Now the honest catch, because a cute German trick is not understanding. These switches set who is speaking and how. None of them sets whether the speaker is telling the truth. The map admits what it did not find. And no, this is not a chat-party trick. The same switches still fire in the raw pretrained model. The switch-hunting framework is promised, not shipped. The atlas itself is public tonight. Your black box just grew a labelled panel. The next time chwen does something weird, you don't re-prompt it. You look it up.
