# Retention Beats (long-form)

Long-form viewers leave at three moments: the first 30 seconds, the first chapter break, and any 30-second stretch with nothing new. Write against all three.

## First 30 seconds

- Sentence one is the hook (a number, a named thing, a wrong diagnosis). Sentence two or three is the promise: what the viewer will be able to do or decide.
- The one surprising number is spoken by 0:20 and shown on screen at the same moment.
- No channel intro, no "today we are going to", no agenda slide.

## Every 30 seconds

- A new piece of information: a number, a named product, a step, a result. If a beat only restates, cut it.
- A visual change at least every 8 seconds is the spec stage's job; give it something to change (a new number, a new step).

## Chapter breaks

- End a chapter on the usable thing, then open the next chapter with why it matters, not with a title read aloud.
- Chapter labels are shown on screen (chapter cards); the narration does not read them.

## Ending

- The last chapter delivers the payoff and a one-line "what to do tonight". No recap of the episode, no spoken call to action (the description and the end card carry the ask).

## Numbers

- At most one new number per sentence; every number names its unit and referent; measured numbers are marked `[measured]` with the expected value and will be rewritten by the capture stage if the measurement differs.
